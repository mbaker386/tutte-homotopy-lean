# Mathematical conventions and correspondence with the manuscript

This document describes the formal development being published, not a claim that
all proposed manuscript changes have already been adopted.

## Scope

The public results are the two-endpoint path theorem and null-homotopy of every
closed off-cut Tutte path in the corrected **local [BJL] formulation**. The formal
development is for finite ground sets and does not assume a finite ambient type.
It is not a formalization of every result in the longer paper, the nine-type
extended homotopy theorem, its foundation applications, Section 4's topological
assertions, or a higher homotopy theorem.

## Meaning-defining conventions

- `Connected` means no partition of the ground set into two nonempty,
  rank-additive parts. Empty and one-element matroids satisfy this convention.
- An indecomposable flat is a flat whose contraction is connected.
- A Tutte path has at least one vertex. Consecutive vertices are distinct
  hyperplanes with an indecomposable corank-two intersection. Repeated
  nonconsecutive vertices and constant paths are allowed.
- The formal theorem permits any modular cut. The elementary configurations
  use the cut with the ground set adjoined. This does not affect off-cut paths
  and makes no difference for a nonempty cut, as assumed in the newer manuscript.
- Selected upper sublattices preserve ambient joins, have the geometric order
  and relative ranks of the finite model, and use intrinsic meets. Arbitrary
  ambient-meet preservation is not required.
- The induced cut is specified on the entire selected finite lattice, not
  merely on its hyperplanes. The third kind is the source's local family,
  not an additional restriction involving all ambient lines.
- Homotopy is generated by finite insertions/deletions of the prescribed
  elementary loops in valid off-cut path contexts. It is not just first-homology
  vanishing and does not require intermediate paths to stay on the initial carrier.

## Proof correspondence

The path theorem follows the corank induction and the Step/Chain/Diamond/Complement
construction. Two supporting connectivity arguments use rank-additive proofs:

1. The converse of Appendix A.1 establishes rank additivity by extending a basis
   and using a separating hyperplane.
2. Appendix A.3 uses persistence of an additive-rank separation under contraction,
   rather than the source's common-cocircuit/component route.

These are recorded local alternatives, not findings that the older source claims
were false. The Diamond proof also omits an incidental, unnecessary assertion
about the auxiliary flat. A separate source-following A.1/A.3 reconciliation is
not present in this snapshot.

The homotopy theorem retains the rank-four counting argument and all four
residual cases of the final descent. The fourth-kind recognition constructs the
actual labelled model, its complete induced cut, and the original loop; it is
not an assumed additional elementary rule. Local repairs include the B.2
separation-side construction, witness/rank clarifications, and the corrected
high-corank descent using inequalities rather than unjustified equalities.

The Special Lemma is conditional on lower-carrier-corank nullity. Its extra-flat
completion uses the earlier pole argument on auxiliary special paths; it does
not invoke the full Special Lemma circularly at the same corank.

Authors should adopt the agreed manuscript replacements and cite the exact
public code snapshot before asserting that the printed arguments themselves
have been formalized. Lean does not check prose-to-code correspondence.
